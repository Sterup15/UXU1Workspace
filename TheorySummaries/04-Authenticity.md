## Why it's a UX topic

Course competency goal: *"Reflect on the impact of authentic storytelling in product narratives."* This sits at the intersection of [Emotional Design](01-Emotional-Design.md)'s **reflective level** (personal/cultural meaning, brand identity) and [Hedonics](05-Hedonics.md)'s **identification/evocation** — authenticity is largely *what makes reflective and identification-level design feel earned rather than manufactured.*

## What "authentic" means here

Two overlapping senses matter for this course:

1. **Authentic *data/feedback*** — in the textbook's usage, authenticity is a property of research: "in-the-wild" studies and rapid design sprints are valued because they give **authentic user feedback** — real behavior in real context, not a guess about what users might want. A/B testing is similarly valued because the situations are "authentic": users act unaware they're part of an evaluation.
2. **Authentic *storytelling/narrative*** — the course's specific phrasing. Storytelling is described in the textbook as one of the most natural ways people communicate ideas and experiences, and it's used in interaction design in (at least) three ways:
   - Participants tell their own stories during data gathering (interviews, observation) — these are extracted and used to communicate findings.
   - Researchers construct stories/scenarios from field data (e.g., ethnographic studies) to feed back into co-design workshops.
   - Designers use **personas**, **scenarios**, and **narrative journey maps** to bring a fictional-but-grounded user's story to life for the design team, capturing goals, motivations, and pain points.
   Including *specific, real* stories (rather than generic ones) is explicitly called out as adding **authenticity and credibility** to findings — video/audio excerpts and direct quotes are hard to dispute in a way that summarized statistics are not.

## Authenticity as a broader design/branding concept

Outside the strict HCI textbook, "authenticity" in product and brand design more broadly usually means the narrative and the product **actually align with each other and with genuine values/origins** — as opposed to a narrative bolted on for marketing effect. Commonly cited dimensions (from branding/marketing theory, e.g., Gilmore & Pine):
- **Genuineness / origin** — is the story true to how the product actually came to be?
- **Consistency / integrity** — does the product's behavior match the story it tells about itself?
- **Transparency** — is the design honest about what it does (contrast with "dark patterns" that manipulate users)?

This connects directly back to the [Emotional Design](01-Emotional-Design.md) case study of Microsoft's *Bob* and *Clippy*: both tried to manufacture warmth/friendliness at the visceral level without a genuinely useful behavioral layer behind it, and users experienced the mismatch as patronizing rather than authentic.

## Design implications

- Ground personas, scenarios, and marketing narratives in *real* user data (interviews, field studies), not assumptions — this is what makes them "authentic" rather than fictional.
- Use real quotes, video/photo excerpts, and specific anecdotes when presenting findings or building a case for a design decision — they carry more credibility than aggregated statistics alone.
- Be wary of designing an emotional "front" (friendly mascots, cheerful copy, comforting colors) that isn't backed by a genuinely well-functioning, honest product — the mismatch reads as inauthentic and can backfire (Clippy).
- Authenticity across platforms means the same brand/product story should feel coherent whether experienced on web, mobile, or a physical product — the course's competency goal explicitly asks students to compare emotional/authentic design across these contexts.

## Key exam talking points

- Distinguish the two senses of "authentic" used in this course: authentic *research data* vs. authentic *storytelling/narrative* in the product itself — and explain how they reinforce each other (authentic data → authentic personas/scenarios → authentic product narrative).
- Use Clippy/Bob as a cautionary example of *inauthentic* emotional design.
- Be ready to discuss how you grounded your own course assignment's persona/narrative in real (or realistic) user data.
